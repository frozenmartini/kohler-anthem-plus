**`Total Water Used` is removed.** It published a field that is not a water meter.

## What `totalFlow` actually is

Across the reference corpus — ten diagnostics captures over two days, both valves — `totalFlow`
took **three distinct values** on one valve (2056.0, 6283.25, 8224.0) and cycled among them
with **no water running and every outlet closed**. It did not move during a measured shower.

And the values come in pairs exactly **4× apart**: 2056/8224, 6488.75/25955, 3659.25/14637. The
cloud serves one underlying number at two different scales, and which one arrives varies per
read.

⚠️ **That 4× is the origin of the 0.7.3 divide-by-four bug.** Two captures a day apart showed a
clean 4.0 ratio and it was read as a unit conversion. It was two members of a repeating set —
so a divisor was right half the time and wrong the other half. Reverted in 0.7.6; now
explained.

As a `total_increasing` sensor, every scale shift read to Home Assistant as a meter
replacement and injected a phantom spike the size of the whole counter into long-term
statistics, which cannot easily be undone once recorded.

## What replaces it

Nothing, because the replacements already shipped:

* **`Water Used This Month`** (0.8.0) — the current month from Kohler's own usage series
* **`Water Used This Year`** (0.13.0) — the last twelve complete months, summed

Both read `gcs-usage`, in litres, the same data the Konnect app charts. Their units were
settled when that endpoint's contract was recovered, rather than inferred from a counter.

## Cleanup

* The entity is **purged from the registry** on the next setup, so it does not linger as a
  permanently unavailable row. Its history goes with it — that history was the phantom spikes.
* The `totalFlow` glitch filter is gone too. It existed to protect the sensor, and it was
  built for the wrong failure: it rejected drops **below half**, while the observed shifts are
  4× and pass straight through.
* **The raw value is still in diagnostics.** It is the evidence for what this field is, which
  remains an open question — just no longer one that affects anyone's statistics.

## The lifetime meter, for the record

`totalVolume` *does* track water: an isolated 2.4 gallon session moved it by 19 counts, making
it eighths of a US gallon to within 1%. But its absolute value is around 537 million, which at
that unit is 67 million gallons — impossible for a shower valve. The high 16 bits were
identical across every reading while only the low bits moved, so something else is packed in
there. No sensor is built on it until that is understood.
