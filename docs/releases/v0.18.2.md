**All three settings write correctly on real hardware** — the first confirmation of that, and
it came with two problems worth fixing rather than living with.

## The entity no longer freezes while a change is checked

Verification has to wait about 30 seconds: Kohler's `gcsadvancestate` is a cloud document that
only updates once the valve reports back, and reading sooner returns the old value. Awaiting
that **inside the service call** meant the slider sat spinning for the whole time. Home
Assistant logs a warning at 10 s, and the entity simply looked broken.

The wait now runs behind the change. The entity responds at once.

**This is not the write-and-trust model, and that is deliberate.** The check is the one thing
the Konnect app does not do, and it exists because a `201` from this endpoint means *accepted
for delivery* — never *applied*. Dropping it would mean a dashboard showing a change the valve
never made. What was actually wrong was **where the waiting happened**, not that it happened.

The POSTs are still awaited. A write the valve refuses, or one that fails part-way and leaves
outlets in mixed state, still fails immediately and visibly — those are fast, and the caller
should hear about them.

A failed *verification* now raises a **repair notice** instead of an exception. That is the
better surface anyway: an exception thrown 30 seconds after someone moved a slider has no one
left to catch it, while a repair notice waits under Settings and says which setting and which
outlets are still holding the old value.

## Default Temperature's slider stops moving underneath you

It used to take its maximum from the current scald limit, which is exactly what the app does —
and in Home Assistant it was the wrong call. **An entity's bounds are cached**, so the slider's
range changed shape under whoever was looking at it, and could show a stale ceiling until the
next update. Combined with the blocking write above, changing `Max Temperature` and then
`Default Temperature` was a confusing sequence.

The slider is now a fixed **59–118 °F**, and a value above the valve's current scald limit is
**refused** with a message naming both numbers and the setting to change:

> 118 °F is above this valve's Max Temperature of 113 °F. A shower cannot start hotter than the
> scald limit — raise Max Temperature first, or pick a lower default.

A fixed range with a clear refusal is more predictable than a range that silently differs from
the one you were just looking at. The limit in force is published as `scald_limit` on the
entity, so it is still visible and usable in automations.

🚨 The rule is enforced **twice, independently**: this entity checks it for the message, and
`GcsDevice.async_write_outlet_config` refuses the same combination before anything reaches the
valve. A default above the scald limit cannot be written even by something that bypasses the
entity entirely.

**Tests: 178 → 179**, including one asserting the write call returns without paying the
verification delay — the regression that would bring the freeze back.
