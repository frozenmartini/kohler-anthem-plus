The temperature slider now covers **92–118 °F**, exactly what the Konnect app's own slider
offers. It was 80–113.

## Both ends were invented here, not taken from the app

The ceiling had a rationale, and the rationale was wrong. The comment read: *"113 °F is also
exactly the `maximumOutletTemperature` the valve reports for every outlet (450 tenths °C), so
the top of the slider matches the hardware's own ceiling."* True of the valve it was written
from — and generalised from that one valve.

The `Max Temperature` sensor added in 0.11.1 disproves it. The owner's two valves report **450
tenths (113 °F) and 477 tenths (117.9 °F)**. So on the second valve the slider was withholding
five degrees the hardware would have accepted, and the app was happy to set.

The floor of 80 °F was never justified at all — it simply predated anyone checking.

**Matching the app is the point.** Those are the numbers on the panel and in the app, and a
Home Assistant control with a different range reads as broken rather than cautious.

## What did not change

* **The valve's own scald limit still governs at any moment** — but it is a *setting*, not a
  hardware ceiling, and can be raised from the app or the panel. The `Max Temperature` sensor
  reports whatever it is now. (An earlier draft of this note said a valve set to 113 "will
  still refuse 118"; see the 0.12.1 notes — that was wrong.)
* **The codec is untouched.** It already encoded the whole 0–48.8 °C range; 118 °F (477 tenths)
  was always representable, and a test now proves it survives encode/decode intact.
* **The `custom_shower` service form moved with the slider** — they are two ways to set the
  same thing, and a form that accepts what the slider refuses is the kind of inconsistency
  nobody finds until it bites. A test now asserts they agree.

On a Celsius account the range is 33–48 °C, which stays inside the 48.8 °C the codec accepts.
