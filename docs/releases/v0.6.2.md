**Changed — the flow reading is no longer treated as untrustworthy when the shower is off.**
`flow_is_live` was documented as deciding whether the flow byte meant anything at all, and
that was too strong a reading of the evidence. It now says only what it can: an outlet is
open and the valve is not paused. The value is always shown.

**Why the original framing was wrong.** It came from the capture corpus, where 296 idle
words carry a flow nobody selected — recurring pairs like 34.5 %/82.5 %, collapsing and
returning within seconds, with `totalFlow` dropping to `2` in the same message. On that
install the idle byte genuinely is noise. But a controller-free K-28210 pair reports idle
bytes that are **stable**: 24.5 % and 26.5 %, one per valve, byte-identical across
diagnostics taken 2 h 23 m apart. Nothing there is transient, and `0x31` / `0x35` read as
stored per-zone settings rather than junk.

Both observations are real and neither generalises to the other install, so the flag is
advisory and the number is presented either way. Hiding it would blank a value that on some
hardware is the valve's actual setting — and a slider with no value cannot be dragged at
all. Whether the difference is firmware, calibration, or the presence of a controller is
unresolved; two installs is not enough to say.

**Added — the Flow number publishes its own bounds.** `minimum_percent`, `maximum_percent`
and `flow_control_available` sit alongside the value. A valve with flow control disabled at
the fixture reports a single-point range, so the slider will not move — and now says why
rather than looking broken.

**Added — a warm-up entity that has never heard from its valve says so.** A valve reporting
no warm-up mode and one set to Off both read blank, and nothing distinguished them. On the
same K-28210 pair, one valve reports `warmUpDisabled` and the other reports nothing at all —
no `warmUpState` in its REST seed and no `GCS_WARM_STS` since. That matters because the
cloud accepts a warm-up command with HTTP 200 whether or not the valve applies it, so a
change made against such a valve can silently do nothing. The entity now carries
`mode_never_reported` and a note saying to watch whether the value sticks.

**Docs — the two idle-flow behaviours are written down.** `docs/architecture.md` gains a
table contrasting them, and both `GcsState.flow_is_live` and the Flow entity carry the
reasoning, so the next person reading either does not have to re-derive why the flag is
advisory.

**Upgrading:** update through HACS and restart Home Assistant. No entities are renamed or
removed and no config changes are needed. The Flow numbers keep their IDs and simply stop
flagging an idle reading as untrustworthy.
