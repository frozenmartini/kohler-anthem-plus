**The Flow control is whole percentages again.** 0.7.4 gave the slider half-percent steps to
match the wire, which resolves to 0.5 % — one byte is half a percent. That is reverted: a
control is for choosing a flow, not for mirroring the device's internal precision, and half
steps doubled the travel across the range to offer a distinction nobody can feel in a shower.

Both the step and the two bounds are whole numbers. The bounds matter as much as the step —
a valve reporting an odd limit byte would put a bound on a half (byte 17 → 8.5 %) and every
position on the slider would inherit it, so the minimum now rounds up and the maximum down,
each away from the value the valve would refuse.

**A half-percent reading is rounded for display.** Both of the owner's valves sit on
half-percent bytes — 24.5 % and 26.5 % — so while water runs the control shows 24 % and 26 %
rather than a value the slider could not return to. Adjusting it then writes the whole
number, moving the real flow by at most 0.5 %.

That trade is deliberate and bounded: it is below anything a person notices, it only happens
when the control is actually used, and every whole percent is exactly representable on the
wire, so nothing is lost on the write path.

**Tests: 42 → 43.** The step and both bounds are asserted to be whole on a constructed
entity, and the display rounding is checked against a real half-percent valve word with an
outlet open — the only condition under which the valve's own reading is the one shown.

**Upgrading:** update through HACS and restart Home Assistant. No entity ids change.
